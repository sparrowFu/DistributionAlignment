015
                     Vision-language representation learning supports multimodal applications by align-
                     ing visual and textual semantics, but existing methods face two challenges. Deter-
016
                     ministic methods embed samples as single points; aligning semantically distinct
017
                     captions to a single image point may compress their differences and cause semantic
018                  distortion. Conversely, while probabilistic methods model samples as distributions,
019                  directly aligning an image distribution with individual caption distributions that
020                  primarily contain semantically similar points can restrict the image’s semantic
021                  range, leading to semantic collapse. To address these challenges, we propose
022                  MCDisp-Align, a probabilistic framework that leverages multi-caption seman-
023                  tic dispersion to guide image distribution learning. MCDisp-Align constructs a
024                  caption-set distribution that captures the shared semantic center, semantic range,
025                  and dominant directions of variation across captions. By combining set-level dis-
026
                     tribution alignment with caption-center coverage, MCDisp-Align aims to reduce
                     semantic distortion by preserving meaning differences between captions, and to
027
                     reduce semantic collapse by guiding the image distribution to cover the meanings
028
                     expressed by those captions. On MSCOCO and Flickr30K, MCDisp-Align achieves
029                  the best or tied-best one-to-many retrieval scores among all compared methods and
030                  improves all six standard retrieval scores over fine-tuned CLIP on MSCOCO. Geo-
031                  metric analysis further shows that caption representations retain variation within
032                  each image while remaining well separated across images, supporting the utility of
033                  caption-set supervision. Code will be released upon acceptance.

036   1      I NTRODUCTION
037
038   Recent advances in large-scale vision-language models have substantially improved multimodal
039   representation learning, enabling visual and textual information to be aligned in a shared semantic
040   space (Li et al., 2021). This progress has supported applications including image-text retrieval (Lee
041   et al., 2018), multimodal understanding (Kim et al., 2021), and other cross-modal tasks. The
042   effectiveness of these applications depends on the quality of semantic alignment: related visual
043   and textual content should be brought together, while meaningful semantic differences should be
044
      preserved and unrelated content should remain well separated.
045   Vision-language representation methods typically use deterministic representations, which map each
046   image and caption to a point in an embedding space and align them via point-to-point matching (Rad-
047   ford et al., 2021). However, as illustrated in Figure 1(a), these captions share the main visual content
048   but differ in their semantic focus. Figure 1(b) shows that aligning these captions with a single image
049   point may compress their semantic distinctions, potentially leading to semantic distortion in the
050
      representation space. In practice, such distortion could reduce sensitivity to fine-grained information:
      retrieval systems may miss captions that emphasize specific attributes or relations, and multimodal
051
      question answering systems may overlook visual evidence needed to distinguish similar answers.
052
053   Probabilistic representation methods offer a more flexible alternative by modeling images and captions
      as distributions (Chun et al., 2025; Venkataramanan et al., 2025). However, distributional modeling

076   alone may not fully capture the semantic differences among an image’s captions. An image contains
077   rich semantic content, including objects, attributes, relations, and background details, whereas an
078   individual caption typically describes only part of this content. The image distribution is intended
079   to accommodate multiple semantic aspects, whereas an individual caption distribution primarily
080   contains points with meanings similar to that of the caption. As shown in Figure 1(c), directly
081
      aligning these distributions without accounting for this difference can restrict the image distribution’s
      semantic range and suppress information beyond an individual caption’s scope. We call this loss
082
      of semantic coverage in the image representation semantic collapse. This motivates using multiple
083
      complementary captions to guide the image distribution to cover their distinct semantic aspects.
084
085   Semantic distortion and semantic collapse highlight a central challenge for vision-language alignment:
086   preserving the semantic diversity across an image’s captions while maintaining separation from other
087
      images. To address this issue, we observe that multiple captions for the same image provide structural
      guidance. While individual captions emphasize different aspects, together they capture three key
088
      properties: a shared semantic center, a semantic range of complementary meanings, and dominant
089
      directions of variation that reflect how these meanings differ.
090
091   Based on this observation, we propose MCDisp-Align, a probabilistic representation learning
092   framework that uses the caption-set structure to guide image distribution learning. Specifically,
093
      MCDisp-Align constructs a caption-set distribution that captures the shared semantic center, semantic
      range, and dominant directions of variation across captions. As illustrated in Figure 1(d), MCDisp-
094
      Align allows the image distribution to cover the different meanings expressed by multiple captions
095
      without directly pulling their text representations toward the same point. This helps preserve semantic
096   differences across captions and reduce semantic distortion. The caption set further provides its
097   semantic range and variation directions to guide image distribution learning, which helps mitigate
098   semantic collapse while keeping different images well separated.
099
      MCDisp-Align consists of three modules. First, the probabilistic representation module maps each
100
      image and caption to a Gaussian distribution, so that the image representation can capture a semantic
101
      range rather than a single point. Second, the caption-set distribution construction module merges
102   the caption distributions of an image into one Gaussian through moment matching. The merged
103   Gaussian captures both the overall center and the spread of the captions, providing a supervision
104   target for the image distribution. Third, the distribution matching and alignment module aligns the
105   image distribution with this target and encourages coverage of each individual caption center, while
106   bidirectional contrastive matching keeps distinct images and caption sets well separated. Since the
107   caption-derived targets are detached, these alignment constraints guide the image distribution without
      directly contracting the caption space, thereby preserving the diversity of caption representations.

140   To mitigate semantic distortion and semantic collapse, MCDisp-Align uses the semantic variation
141   across an image’s captions to guide image distribution learning. As shown in Figure 2, MCDisp-Align
142   consists of three modules: (1) Probabilistic Representation maps images and individual captions
143   to Gaussian distributions, whose means represent semantic centers and covariances model variation
144   around them; (2) Caption-Set Distribution Construction aggregates all caption distributions of an
145   image via moment matching, summarizing their shared center, per-dimension spread, and principal
146
      directions of variation; and (3) Distribution Matching and Alignment distinguishes matching
      image-caption groups while using these statistics to supervise the image mean, diagonal variance, and
147
      low-rank covariance. A caption-level coverage constraint further encourages the image distribution
148
      to include the centers of its individual captions.
149

271
272   We use MSCOCO (Lin et al., 2014) and Flickr30K (Young et al., 2014), each with five reference
273   captions per image. Our baselines include deterministic model CLIP (Radford et al., 2021) and
274   probabilistic models ProLIP (Chun et al., 2025) and GroVE (Venkataramanan et al., 2025). CLIP and
275   ProLIP are evaluated in zero-shot and fine-tuned settings; FT denotes fine-tuning on the target dataset.
276   MCDisp-Align, CLIP, and GroVE use ViT-L/14, while ProLIP uses ViT-H/14 (Dosovitskiy et al.,
277   2020). All methods use the same image pools, with text candidates defined by the retrieval setting.
278   We fine-tune the encoders and train the MCDisp-Align distribution heads with a batch size of 32 and
279   a head learning rate of 5 × 10−5 . We stop training after three consecutive epochs without validation
280   improvement and retain the best checkpoint. Images and captions are encoded independently at
281   inference time. We report Recall@1/5/10 (R@1/5/10) for image-to-text (I2T) and text-to-image (T2I)
282   retrieval as fractions, with differences expressed in percentage points.

440
441   4.1   V ISION - LANGUAGE REPRESENTATION LEARNING
442
443   Common vision-language architectures include fusion-based models and dual encoders. UNITER
444   learns joint representations through modality fusion (Chen et al., 2020), while CLIP and ALIGN use
445   separate encoders trained with large-scale contrastive learning (Radford et al., 2021; Jia et al., 2021;
446   Lu et al., 2025). SigLIP replaces the softmax loss with a pairwise sigmoid loss (Zhai et al., 2023),
447   and SigLIP 2 extends it to multilingual and fine-grained settings (Tschannen et al., 2025).
448   Beyond global alignment, local alignment methods combine image and text tokens (Asokan et al.,
449   2025), use text-conditioned pooling (Xiao et al., 2025), or train with region-text pairs and hard
450   negatives (Xie et al., 2025; 2026). GOAL, β-CLIP, and TIPSv2 match images and text at multiple
451   levels of detail (Choi et al., 2025; Zohra et al., 2026; Cao et al., 2026). Other methods exploit visual
452   structure (Ruan et al., 2026) or long captions (Lavoie et al., 2026), while FALCON reduces false
453   negatives in one-to-many matching through adaptive negative mining (Kim et al., 2026).
454   MCDisp-Align shares the dual-encoder design and the goal of preserving fine-grained semantics,
455   focusing on variation among an image’s valid captions. It uses caption-set dispersion to supervise the
456   image distribution’s semantic range while retaining independent encoding for standard retrieval.
457
458
459
      4.2   P ROBABILISTIC VISION - LANGUAGE REPRESENTATION LEARNING
460
461
      Probabilistic methods model ambiguity by mapping inputs to distributions. PCME introduces prob-
      abilistic embeddings (Chun et al., 2021), which PCME++ improves using a closed-form distance
462
      and better false-negative handling (Chun, 2024). Probabilistic representations have been expanded
463
      to composed retrieval (Tang et al., 2025), large-scale pre-training (Chun et al., 2025), probabilistic
464   adapters (Upadhyay et al., 2023), and latent variable models (Venkataramanan et al., 2025). Other
465   methods estimate uncertainty without learning new embeddings, using Bayesian projection lay-
466   ers (Baumann et al., 2026) or class-conditional Gaussian distributions (Lin et al., 2026). To account
467   for normalized embeddings, AsymVLM models asymmetric uncertainty on the unit sphere (Ju et al.,
468   2025), and REPVLM estimates uncertainty using density models (Ju et al., 2026).
469
      MCDisp-Align uses variation across an image’s captions to supervise its distribution, combining
470   within-caption uncertainty and between-caption dispersion through moment matching. The resulting
471   targets guide the image’s semantic center, range, and dominant variation directions. Detaching these
472   targets allows set-level alignment and caption-center coverage to shape the image distribution without
473   directly pulling caption centers together.
474

476   5     C ONCLUSION
477
478
      In this paper, we present MCDisp-Align, which uses multi-caption semantic dispersion to guide
479
      image distribution learning for one-to-many vision-language alignment. Caption-set supervision helps
480   reduce semantic distortion by shaping the image distribution without directly pulling semantically
481   distinct caption centers together. Set-level alignment and caption-center coverage help mitigate
482   semantic collapse by guiding the image distribution to cover the complementary meanings of its
483   captions. On MSCOCO and Flickr30K, MCDisp-Align achieves the best or tied-best one-to-many
484   retrieval scores among the methods, while retaining performance in standard image-text retrieval and
485   outperforming fine-tuned CLIP on MSCOCO. Caption-space analysis and ablations show that these
      gains coexist with retained caption variation within images and separation across images.
